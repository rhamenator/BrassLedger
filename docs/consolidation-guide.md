# Consolidation and currency reporting

BrassLedger's current consolidation report is a controlled as-of balance foundation. It derives each member company's natural account balances from posted journal lines through the requested date. It does not read today's mutable account balance for a historical report, and it retains inactive accounts that had historical activity.

## Consolidation basis and ownership periods

Create a consolidation group in **Administration → Consolidation and currencies** only when the active operator is an owner of every selected company. Give each initial member an effective-from date, optional effective-through date, ownership percentage above 0% and no more than 100%, and an explicit consolidation basis. A classified group has exactly one **Reporting parent** at 100%. Every other classified member requires a dated review conclusion and rationale.

The basis controls inclusion independently from economic ownership:

- **Reporting parent** includes 100% of the parent's balances.
- **Controlled subsidiary** includes 100% of the subsidiary's balances. A parent interest below 100% also requires separate noncontrolling-interest presentation for each exact reporting period.
- **Combined affiliate** includes 100% for a reviewed common-control combination that is being presented on a combined basis.
- **Proportionate interest** applies the retained ownership percentage. Use it only when proportionate presentation is the reviewed accounting conclusion, not as a shortcut for a partially owned controlled subsidiary.

This distinction follows the control principle in [IFRS 10](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-10-consolidated-financial-statements/): control determines which entities are consolidated. The separate NCI policy follows the presentation requirements summarized in [IFRS 10](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/pdf-standards/english/2022/issued/part-a/ifrs-10-consolidated-financial-statements.pdf?bypass=on) and [FASB Statement 160](https://storage.fasb.org/aop_fas160.pdf). These links are accounting references, not a substitute for the entity's documented framework conclusion or independent professional review.

Ownership is retained as periods rather than a replaceable current percentage. Periods for the same group and company cannot overlap. Closing or correcting a period requires its current concurrency token; a stale screen cannot overwrite another operator's change. Adding or revising a period also changes the group concurrency token so concurrent ownership changes serialize. Every accepted group and ownership change creates a company-scoped business-audit event. Historical reports select the ownership period effective on their as-of date.

Existing undated memberships are migrated with an open period beginning at the minimum supported date, nonblank concurrency tokens, and the legacy **Proportionate interest** basis. Legacy rows are not silently asserted to be controlled or combined. Until an operator records the appropriate rationale and review date, reports identify the legacy basis as unreviewed and incomplete.

## Controlled translation behavior

Before relying on a report, choose **Map accounts** for the group and map every member-company source account with a material balance to an explicit reporting account number and name. Mapping periods are effective-dated and cannot overlap for the same source account. A reporting number must retain one name and account type during overlapping periods, preventing unrelated accounts from being merged merely because their local numbers happen to match. The source company, source account, reporting identity, dates, status, concurrency token, operator, and audit event remain retained. Close a mapping with an effective-through date before adding its successor; deactivation also requires an end date so past reports remain reproducible.

The report uses only the mapping effective on its as-of date. An unmapped or multiply mapped nonzero balance is excluded with a warning that identifies the company, source account, source-currency amount, and reason. A zero balance does not create noise. Inactive source accounts remain available because they may contain historical activity. Account mappings classify balances but do not alter any member company's chart or ledger.

Each account mapping retains an explicit translation method. New asset and liability mappings default to **Closing**, revenue and expense mappings default to **Average**, and equity mappings default to **Historical**. The operator can deliberately choose another method, but one reporting account cannot use inconsistent methods during overlapping mapping periods.

Maintain rates as separately typed evidence:

- A closing rate is the latest closing observation effective on or before the report date. It converts the applicable account balance at the report date.
- An average rate covers an explicit start and end date. Each included nominal-account journal line uses the average period covering its posting date. Average periods for one directed currency pair cannot overlap.
- A historical rate is the latest historical observation effective on or before each applicable journal line's posting date. Equity is therefore not translated at today's closing rate.

Every rate retains its type, period, source label, optional source reference, retrieval date, approval state, concurrency token, operator, and audit event. Only an active-company owner can maintain rates. Stale corrections are rejected. BrassLedger does not fall back from a missing average or historical rate to a closing rate, and it does not silently choose between conflicting direct and inverse rate series. Same-currency translation uses one without requiring a rate.

The report accepts a report-period start and as-of date; callers that omit the start use the active parent company's fiscal-year start. Average-translated nominal activity before the period start is excluded. If the resulting source-currency selection does not balance—usually because nominal activity was not closed before the selected period—BrassLedger reports the source imbalance and refuses to disguise it as CTA. Missing, ambiguous, unmapped, or type-inconsistent material balances are likewise excluded with precise warnings, and CTA is not calculated for an incomplete report.

Configure a dedicated CTA reporting account number and name on the consolidation group. When every included member selection balances in its source currency, BrassLedger computes the reporting-currency imbalance created solely by the different controlled translation methods and inserts that amount as an equity balance. The CTA number cannot also be used by a source-account mapping. The report exposes the calculated adjustment and translation method on each row, and rounds basis-adjusted account results to cents.

## Noncontrolling interests

Configure a dedicated NCI account number and name in the group's **Policy**. It must be a separate equity reporting identity and cannot collide with CTA or an effective source-account mapping. BrassLedger fully includes a partially owned controlled subsidiary; it does not multiply that subsidiary's balances by the parent's ownership percentage.

For each partially owned controlled subsidiary and exact report period, a preparer must enter the reviewed, balanced equity reclassification under **Reporting → Consolidation adjustments and eliminations → Noncontrolling-interest reclassification**. The batch retains the subject subsidiary, its exact period, line-level source provenance, preparation, independent review, posting, reversal, concurrency, and audit evidence. Every line must be equity, must identify that same subsidiary, and exactly one line must use the dedicated NCI account. A unique period-and-subsidiary control prevents duplicate retained NCI batches, including concurrent attempts.

BrassLedger deliberately does not guess the NCI amount from ownership percentage. Use the ownership-event workspace described below for reviewed acquisition-date measurement, profit/OCI attribution, ownership changes, and loss-of-control schedules. Without an exact-period posted NCI batch, the report warns that NCI presentation is missing, treats the result as incomplete, and does not calculate CTA. Reversal posts an exact opposite batch and releases the control identity so a corrected replacement can be prepared without deleting history.

## Acquisition, disposal, ownership-change, and attribution schedules

Use **Reporting → Acquisitions, disposals, ownership changes and attribution** to retain the accounting conclusion and balanced consolidation-only posting together. Supported event types are acquisition of control, step acquisition, ownership change without loss of control, loss of control/disposal, and profit/OCI attribution. Each event retains its subject company, accounting date, ownership before and after, framework and edition, measurement rationale, working-paper source, schema version, typed measurement, posting lines, arbitrary JSON extensions, exact-content SHA-256, concurrency token, and full control history.

The versioned JSON schedule avoids fixing every future accounting field in relational columns. Schema 1 applies these controls:

- An acquisition reconciles consideration transferred, a previously held interest at fair value, recognized NCI, and identifiable net assets to exactly one of goodwill or bargain purchase gain. US GAAP acquisitions with recognized NCI require the full-fair-value method; IFRS schedules may explicitly select full fair value or proportionate share as supported by the retained framework conclusion.
- An ownership change without loss of control reconciles consideration paid/received, the NCI carrying-amount increase/decrease, and the parent-equity debit/credit as an equity transaction.
- A loss of control reconciles proceeds, retained-interest fair value, derecognized NCI, net assets, goodwill, and signed OCI reclassification to the recorded gain or loss.
- A profit/OCI attribution separately reconciles each subsidiary total to the parent and NCI amounts. It does not multiply current-period income by ownership percentage without a reviewed schedule.
- Every posting has 2 through 500 lines, uses mapped reporting accounts effective on the original event date, has exactly one positive debit or credit per line, and balances exactly to cents. CTA remains system-controlled. Revenue or expense postings require a mapped equity carryforward account.

The ownership effective on the accounting date must agree with the event. An acquisition from 0% is dated on the first controlled-subsidiary day. A step acquisition is dated on that same first controlled day, matches the positive immediately preceding noncontrolled ownership period, and separately measures the previously held interest at fair value. A continuing-control ownership change is dated on a successor controlled period and must match the immediately preceding controlled percentage. A loss of control is dated on the controlled period's final day. Profit attribution uses the unchanged percentage effective on its accounting date. These are consistency checks, not assertions that a percentage alone establishes control. The preparer cannot approve or reject their event, and the approver cannot post it. A posted event is immutable. Reversal requires its own date and reason, creates an exact opposite posted event linked to the original, and never changes a member ledger.

Posted balance-sheet lines remain in every later consolidated report. Revenue and expense lines appear in the event's reporting period; in later periods the system replaces their net result with the event's reviewed retained-earnings account so the balance sheet remains balanced instead of silently dropping prior-period acquisition or disposal income. Reports revalidate the stored JSON, formulas, posting balance, and event-date account mappings and exclude a corrupt event with an explicit incompleteness warning.

Current and comparative JSON, CSV, Excel, and PDF statement packages retain every valid posted event whose balance-sheet, current-period, reversal, or prior-period carryforward contribution is reflected in that package, including an acquisition schedule from an earlier period when its posting still affects the current balance sheet. The exported schedule includes the typed measurements, balanced posting, ownership percentages, framework edition, source reference, preparation/approval/posting identities and times, schema version, and exact-content SHA-256. A member entering or leaving the group, or changing ownership/basis during the period, keeps the package incomplete unless the corresponding posted acquisition, loss-of-control, or ownership-change schedule exists.

This model follows the acquisition-method and residual-goodwill principles summarized by [IFRS 3](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/publications/pdf-standards/english/2022/issued/part-a/ifrs-3-business-combinations.pdf?bypass=on) and [FASB Statement 141(R)](https://fasb.org/page/PageContent?bcpath=tff&pageId=%2Freference-library%2Fsuperseded-standards%2Fsummary-of-statement-no-141-revised-2007.html), and the equity-transaction, attribution, retained-interest fair-value, and deconsolidation principles summarized by [IFRS 10](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ifrs-10-consolidated-financial-statements/) and [FASB Statement 160](https://fasb.org/page/PageContent?bcpath=tff&pageId=%2Freference-library%2Fsuperseded-standards%2Fsummary-of-statement-no-160.html). The FASB pages summarize requirements now codified in ASC 805 and ASC 810; the stored framework edition and independent accounting review remain authoritative for a particular transaction.

## Consolidated statement package and cash-flow classification

**Reporting → Run statement package** derives a consolidated balance sheet, income statement, statement of changes in equity, and statement of cash flows from the same exact-period translated balance and posted consolidation entries. The package exposes source-company and source-account contributions beneath each mapped account, presents current-period earnings explicitly within equity, and reports separate balance-sheet, equity, and cash reconciliation differences. A package with any source warning or nonzero reconciliation is visibly **Incomplete**.

Cash and cash equivalents are identified from each member company's retained bank-account ledger links and their effective consolidation mappings; BrassLedger does not guess from account numbers or names. For direct cash-flow classification, configure the **noncash counterpart** mapping as Operating, Investing, or Financing. Every classified mapping requires a concise rationale and review date, is effective-dated with the reporting mapping, uses optimistic concurrency, and is retained in the business audit. A bank ledger mapping must remain Unclassified because it identifies cash rather than the nature of the transaction. A cash journal can be split across multiple classified counterpart lines, consistent with the principle that one transaction can contain differently classified flows.

Use **Administration → Consolidation and currencies → Present statements** to assign every material balance-sheet and income-statement reporting identity to a section and line caption. Each policy retains a free-form section code and caption, section and line sort order, rationale, review date, effective period, concurrency token, and business-audit event. This supports current/noncurrent splits and later presentation changes without changing source-account mappings or rewriting prior-period output. A section code must keep one caption and order during overlapping periods, and policies for one statement account cannot overlap. Nonzero accounts without exactly one effective reviewed policy appear in a clearly named unconfigured section and keep the package Incomplete; the system never guesses current/noncurrent classification.

To compare periods, enter current and comparison date ranges and choose **Compare statement periods**. BrassLedger generates both statement packages independently under the membership, ownership, exchange rate, mapping, presentation, adjustment, and warning policies effective in each period. The four comparison tables report current, comparison, and current-minus-comparison variance. They retain both periods' section and line captions, so a reclassification remains visible instead of silently restating the earlier period. The comparison date must precede the current as-of date, and either period's warning keeps the combined package Incomplete.

The three cash-flow activity categories follow [IAS 7 Statement of Cash Flows](https://www.ifrs.org/issued-standards/list-of-standards/ias-7-statement-of-cash-flows.html/) and the operating, investing, and financing model in [FASB Topic 230 materials](https://storage.fasb.org/FASAC-ALL-201603%20All%20Materials%20Public.pdf). Classification and statement presentation must still reflect the reporting framework and nature of the business; these links and the software policies do not replace professional review.

Member cash flows use the period-average rate covering the journal date and the member's effective consolidation basis. Unclassified counterpart activity remains separately visible with the exact company, account, and journal reference. The statement presents the residual between classified translated flows and the opening-to-closing cash change as exchange-rate, consolidation-entry, and rounding effects, then reconciles back to reported cash. Noncash journals are excluded. Controlled CSV, Excel, and PDF downloads are available for both current and comparative packages. Excel includes separate summary, statement, and source-detail sheets; PDF includes wrapped, paginated statement and source tables with repeated headers. Every format retains dates, currency, completion state, warnings, reconciliation, and source provenance. Incomplete documents carry an explicit warning against external use.

## Versioned framework disclosures

Use **Reporting → Framework disclosures** to prepare one package for an exact consolidation group, period, and reporting framework. The framework menu suggests US GAAP, IFRS, and IFRS for SMEs but accepts a concise custom code. Each package retains its framework edition, JSON schema version, typed content, preparation/review notes, preparer, independent approver or rejector, decision reason, concurrency token, company-scoped audit events, and a SHA-256 digest of the exact stored JSON in its audit and output metadata. An approved package is immutable. Correct a rejected package in place; create a distinct period or framework package when the reporting identity changes.

The JSON document is deliberately separate from ledger tables. Schema version 1 has three typed sections:

- **Financing liabilities** reconcile opening balance to closing balance through financing cash flows, acquisitions, disposals, foreign-exchange changes, fair-value changes, and other noncash changes. Reductions are negative; any other noncash movement requires an explanation. Every row retains its balance-sheet presentation and source reference.
- **Supplier-finance arrangements** reconcile opening obligations plus confirmed obligations less paid obligations to closing obligations. Each row retains key terms, balance-sheet presentation, amounts for suppliers already paid, arrangement and comparable-payable due-date ranges, security/guarantees, liquidity-risk notes, and source evidence.
- **Other disclosures** use a category menu for common notes—such as accounting policies, going concern, subsequent events, related parties, commitments, debt/covenants, liquidity, taxes, leases, revenue, inventory, fixed assets, benefits, EPS, segments, and fair value—while also accepting custom categories. Arbitrary future attributes can be retained in each record's `extensions` object without a database migration.

All currency inputs are cent-precision and bounded to the application's supported money range. Codes are unique within their section, due-date ranges are validated, and reconciliation failures are rejected before retention. A preparer cannot approve or reject their own work. Only an independently approved exact-period package appears in statements or downloads; a retained Draft or Rejected package produces an explicit statement warning instead of being published. Current and comparative JSON, CSV, Excel, PDF, and browser output preserve each period's approved packages independently. Excel adds one worksheet per framework and period; PDF adds wrapped disclosure pages.

The financing-liability objective and reconciliation model are based on [IAS 7 paragraph 44A implementation guidance](https://www.ifrs.org/content/dam/ifrs/supporting-implementation/agenda-decisions/2019/ias-7-disclosure-of-changes-in-liabilities-arising-from-financing-activities-september-2019.pdf). Supplier-finance fields reflect the transparency objectives of the [IAS 7 and IFRS 7 supplier-finance amendments](https://www.ifrs.org/news-and-events/news/2023/05/iasb-increases-transparency-of-companies-supplier-finance/) and the terms, balance-sheet presentation, outstanding amounts, and rollforward required by [FASB ASU 2022-04](https://storage.fasb.org/ASU%202022-04.pdf). These structures support preparation and review; they do not assert that a package contains every disclosure required for a particular entity, industry, transaction, or framework edition.

## Controlled adjustments and eliminations

Use **Reporting → Consolidation adjustments and eliminations** for reporting-only entries. Each retained batch belongs to one group and an exact period-start/as-of pair; it never changes a member company's general ledger. Lines must use reporting identities established by mappings effective on the as-of date, contain exactly one positive debit or credit within the supported currency range, balance to the cent, and cannot target the system-controlled CTA account. Posting or reversing is blocked when any parent-company accounting period overlaps the reporting range and remains closed.

A preparer can save or correct a Draft or Rejected batch. A different operator must approve or reject it, and the approver cannot post it. Every transition requires its displayed concurrency token. Rejection requires a correction reason. Posted batches are immutable: reversal creates and posts an exact opposite batch, retains both records and their linkage, and marks the original Reversed. Preparation, revision, decision, posting, and reversal create company-scoped business-audit events.

An **Intercompany elimination** additionally requires a match reference and, on every line, two different companies that are effective members on the report date. A manual adjustment cannot carry intercompany-only provenance. Reports revalidate retained balance, account, date, CTA, and company-pair invariants. A corrupt or no-longer-supported batch is excluded with a warning and prevents CTA from concealing the incomplete selection.

Configure reciprocal, effective-dated customer and vendor links with **Administration → Consolidation and currencies → Trading partners**. Only an active owner of the parent and both linked member companies can create or close a link, and the link period must have continuous retained ownership coverage for both companies (it may cross adjacent ownership-percentage periods). A retained link cannot be reassigned to another record; close it and create a successor so historical discovery remains reproducible.

**Reporting → Reviewed intercompany matches** discovers only unambiguous posted invoice/bill pairs whose configured reciprocal links are effective on each document date and whose trimmed references and total amounts match exactly. Discovery currently requires both companies to have the same base currency because source documents do not yet retain transaction currency. Ambiguous and cross-currency candidates remain unlinked and produce warnings. Suggestions and refreshed open balances are retained; an operator can exclude one with an audited reason, restore it, or use it to prepare an elimination. Using a suggestion preserves its generated match reference and exact company pair, but deliberately does not infer accounts, debit/credit direction, or post anything. The operator must choose and balance effective reporting accounts, and the normal independent approval and posting controls still apply.

The consolidated balance includes only batches posted for the requested exact period. Original and reversal entries are both included, producing a transparent zero net effect after reversal. When translated source activity and reporting adjustments use the same reporting identity, the report combines the value and labels its derivation **Mixed**.

This remains short of a framework-complete consolidated-financial-statement workflow. Broader fuzzy/partial/settlement and cross-currency matching, controlled elimination-line derivation, transaction-specific professional review of acquisition/disposal schedules, and an independently reviewed framework/industry disclosure checklist remain required. Do not distribute the statement package as framework-compliant consolidated financial statements until those controls and an independent accounting review are complete.
